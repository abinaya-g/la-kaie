\opening{Dear Editors,}

I submit the manuscript \emph{``EIG-MPHBS: a rotation-invariant information exchange for hybrid Honey Badger--Marine
Predators optimisation''} for consideration as a research article in \emph{Expert Systems with Applications}.

Hybrid metaheuristics are widely used as the optimisation engine of intelligent systems in engineering. Many of them let
two populations cooperate by recombining their members coordinate by coordinate. The manuscript shows that this
exchange depends on the coordinate system and fails on problems whose variables are coupled. It does so for MPHBS, a
recent hybrid of the Honey Badger Algorithm and the Marine Predators Algorithm (Flores and Olivares,
\emph{Knowledge-Based Systems} 352, 2026). It then fixes the weakness by carrying out the exchange in the eigenvector
frame of the pooled populations.

The manuscript does not propose a new metaphor-based algorithm. The method is stated in standard optimisation
terminology (covariance estimation, eigenvector frame, coordinate-wise recombination). It is positioned against the
eigenvector-based crossover and multitasking literature it builds on, and its properties are proved formally. The main
contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a proof that the eigen-frame exchange is invariant to rotations of the search space and costs no extra function
  evaluations, with a measured run-time overhead of 3--6\%;
\item a mechanism analysis showing that the eigen frame restores the acceptance of exchanged candidates on coupled
  problems, where the axis-frame exchange stalls;
\item an evaluation in 18\,840 seed-paired runs on CEC2022, the 24 BBOB functions and eight FIR filter-design problems
  against ten algorithms: MPHBS, its parent algorithms, PSO, DE, GWO, WOA, L-SHADE and CMA-ES, plus the official
  CEC2022 results of EA4eig;
\item a design fixed before the experiments, with confirmatory tests on held-out instances, and public code, raw runs
  and analysis scripts.
\end{itemize}

The results are reported in full, including their limits. EIG-MPHBS is never significantly worse than MPHBS on any of
56 instances and clearly outperforms the classical metaheuristics, but L-SHADE, CMA-ES and EA4eig remain stronger. The
FIR filter-design problems provide the engineering application.

The manuscript cites one related manuscript by the author, which is under review elsewhere. It is cited anonymously as
``Anonymous (2026)'' and identified on the title page. That manuscript shows that the SARSA learner of MPHBS gives no
benefit over a random choice at the same budget. The present submission builds on this finding: it uses the
random-mediator variant as a baseline, in new runs, and its contribution, the eigen-frame exchange and its analysis,
does not appear in that manuscript. I am glad to provide a copy if useful.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for publication
elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests, and the research received no specific funding.

Thank you for considering this submission.
