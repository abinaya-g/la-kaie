\begin{letter}{The Editors\\Applied Soft Computing}

\opening{Dear Editors,}

I submit the manuscript \emph{``Learned versus structural information exchange in two-population hybrid optimisers:
matched controls, credit-assignment limits and a rotation-invariant exchange''} for consideration as a research article in
\emph{Applied Soft Computing}.

Hybrid optimisers that run two populations side by side share information by recombining their members, and some steer
this exchange with a learned controller. The manuscript asks what such a controller adds and what limits the exchange, and
it answers with theory and three studies.

The main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a decomposition of any learned-versus-control contrast into a learning, a budget and a state term, with conditions
  under which a control measures learning alone, and a protocol to build and verify such controls;
\item two properties derived from the update rule of a SARSA value table, one on credit assignment and one on its noise
  floor, which bound what the controller can learn, with a logged-run check of both;
\item a pre-registered study with matched controls (14\,300 seed-paired runs on CEC2022 and eight FIR filter-design
  problems, plus a BBOB extension) in which a uniform random choice performs at least as well as the learned controller;
\item an exchange performed in the eigenvector frame of the pooled populations, which is invariant to rotations of the
  search space and uses no extra function evaluations, evaluated on 56 instances against ten algorithms, with a
  14-fold reduction of the median error on the rotated ellipsoid;
\item a pre-registered transfer study (3\,840 runs) showing that the same exchange improves two further parent pairs,
  strongly for particle swarm with differential evolution and slightly for grey wolf with whale optimisation.
\end{itemize}

Together, these results give a checkable standard for evaluating learned components in search algorithms and show how a
structural change to the exchange step improves hybrid optimisers at no evaluation cost. The limits of the approach,
including its failure on rotated multimodal functions and the gap to CMA-ES and L-SHADE, are reported. All code, the
frozen pre-registrations, the run records and the analysis scripts are publicly available.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere.
The author declares no competing interests, and the research received no specific funding. Thank you for considering this
submission.
